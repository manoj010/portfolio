# Proof by Contradiction: A Simple Guide

Proof by contradiction is a method of mathematical proof where you prove a statement by assuming the opposite is true, then showing that this assumption leads to an impossible conclusion.

Once the opposite assumption collapses, the original statement must be true.

This style of proof is useful because some mathematical ideas are hard to prove directly. Instead of building the statement from the ground up, contradiction lets us ask: what would go wrong if this statement were false?

---

## The General Structure

Suppose we want to prove a statement P.

The proof usually follows this pattern:

* Assume P is false.
* Use definitions, logic, and known facts to explore the consequences.
* Derive a contradiction.
* Conclude that the assumption must be false.
* Therefore, P is true.

A contradiction can look like:

* A statement and its negation, such as x > 5 and x <= 5.
* A violation of a known theorem or definition.
* An impossible conclusion, such as 0 = 1.

The important idea is that the contradiction does not prove the false assumption. It destroys the false assumption.

---

## Classic Example: The Square Root of 2 Is Irrational

We want to prove that the square root of 2 is irrational.

In other words, we want to prove that sqrt(2) cannot be written as a fraction of two integers.

## Step 1: Assume the Opposite

Assume sqrt(2) is rational.

Then it can be written as a fraction in lowest terms:

```text
sqrt(2) = a / b
```

where a and b are integers, b is not 0, and a and b have no common factor other than 1.

## Step 2: Square Both Sides

Starting with:

```text
sqrt(2) = a / b
```

Square both sides:

```text
2 = a^2 / b^2
```

Multiply both sides by b^2:

```text
a^2 = 2b^2
```

This means a^2 is even.

If a^2 is even, then a must also be even.

So we can write:

```text
a = 2k
```

for some integer k.

## Step 3: Substitute Back

Substitute a = 2k into the equation a^2 = 2b^2:

```text
(2k)^2 = 2b^2
4k^2 = 2b^2
b^2 = 2k^2
```

This means b^2 is even.

If b^2 is even, then b must also be even.

## Step 4: Find the Contradiction

We have shown that both a and b are even.

That means both share a factor of 2.

But we started by saying that a / b was in lowest terms.

That is a contradiction.

## Step 5: Conclude

The assumption that sqrt(2) is rational must be false.

Therefore:

```text
sqrt(2) is irrational.
```

## A Simpler Example

Let us prove that there is no largest positive integer.

Assume the opposite: suppose there is a largest positive integer N.

Now consider:

```text
N + 1
```

Since N + 1 is also a positive integer, and N + 1 is greater than N, we have found a positive integer larger than the supposed largest positive integer.

That is impossible.

Therefore, there is no largest positive integer.

## When to Use Proof by Contradiction

Proof by contradiction is especially useful when you are trying to prove:

* Something does not exist.
* A number is irrational.
* A set is infinite.
* A mathematical object is unique.
* A direct proof feels awkward or unclear.

Famous examples include:

* The square root of 2 is irrational.
* There are infinitely many prime numbers.
* No rational number squared equals 2.
* Certain results in geometry, number theory, and set theory.

---

## Comparison With Other Proof Methods

| Method | Main Idea |
| --- | --- |
| Direct proof | Start with the assumptions and derive the conclusion. |
| Contrapositive | To prove "if P, then Q", prove "if not Q, then not P". |
| Contradiction | Assume the statement is false and derive an impossibility. |

Contradiction is powerful because it lets you transform a difficult claim into a logical stress test.

Instead of asking, "How do I prove this directly?", you ask, "What breaks if this were false?"

---

## Final Thought

Proof by contradiction is one of the most elegant tools in mathematics.

It works because logic is unforgiving: if an assumption leads to an impossibility, the assumption cannot be true.

That is the entire beauty of the method.
